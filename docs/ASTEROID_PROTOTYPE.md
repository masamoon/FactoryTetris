# Asteroid Works — playable implementation

Asteroid Works is the bounded side-view factory experiment begun on 2026-09-20. The 2026-09-23 pacing, touch and rendering changes are recorded in [the review](reviews/2026-09-23-pacing-touch-performance.md). This prototype is not a validated long-term game.

## Current loop

1. Hold three exposed plain-rock blocks for six ore. Plain rock takes 12 real simulation ticks per block; rich ore takes 24. Manual work remains capped at one unit per tick.
2. Build a six-ore shaft drill in cleared space. It excavates eight real cells and returns finite ore loads to its stationary left output. Its corridor, rail travel, eight-item capacity and backpressure remain real.
3. Connect free directional belts to COLLECTION. The authored starter line uses a drill at x7/y6 and belts x2–6/y6. A transfer moves cargo at most one cell.
4. Spend eight ore on a smelter, then six plates on an assembler. Each two ore becomes one plate; each two plates becomes one part. Belts supply their right inputs and carry left outputs. A shared service drone can deliver a single real batch from COLLECTION as manual recovery.
5. Expand with ore: a second drill costs two ore, a third eight ore, each with its own output route. An earned part can reserve an eight-cell extension after a shaft clears. The tender waits for that drill's old deep pocket and returning loads to empty, then visibly installs the section. A queued kit can be cancelled before launch for one part back.

Fixed deep pockets exist at world cells (15,6): 96 ore, (16,5): 48, (16,7): 120 and (23,6): 72. A drill extracts them in real four-ore loads after clearing the preceding eight-cell shaft. They persist independently of drills, are not refilled by construction and are surveyed in the build preview and shown as remaining ore at the face and base. Other drill sites still excavate their finite surface terrain. One extension globally and three bases are the current experiment caps. These pockets extend useful production through the first processing decisions; they do not provide endless supply.

## Touch, planning and recovery

The 2026-09-25 mobile-first interface is recorded in [its adversarial review](reviews/2026-09-25-mobile-ui-adversarial-review.md). The canvas is full-screen; a stock pill, goal chip and menu float above it and one contextual dock floats below. The scene keeps the world between them rather than resizing the canvas. Build tools appear once the first ore or machine exists; live mining is the default mode, so there is no Mine or Remove tool.

Hold a block to mine and drag elsewhere to pan. Pinch or scroll to zoom; two fingers pan in any mode, and a belt drag near a screen edge scrolls the camera. A recentre control appears only when collection is out of view or the zoom has changed. Select a tool, tap to preview, then tap the ghost again or press Build. Planning pauses the simulation and the dock labels it “time stopped”; Done, re-tapping the active tool, building a machine or completing a dragged conveyor route resumes it. Single tapped conveyors stay in planning for precise work. Drill mode highlights sites whose shaft reaches a surveyed pocket; processor modes highlight replaceable empty conveyors. Tap a machine to inspect its real buffers, pocket and status; tap a conveyor to rotate or remove it. For a one-gesture manual feed, drag from COLLECTION to an eligible processor or tap its on-world FEED action. The drone removes actual stock at dispatch, reserves input space, travels on the live clock and transfers the same cargo only at arrival; it returns before another dispatch. Belts remain the durable supply path. Each real delivery to collection shows a brief “+1” above it; no feedback is shown for cargo that has not arrived.

Complete-state undo rewinds subsequent production and terrain along with the edit. Up to 40 checkpoints fit within the 2 MB save budget. Construction, cancellation and courier dispatch save immediately; production saves at most once per second while ticks change. Saves use a separate namespace from the tile workshop. Older asteroid saves gain the fixed geological pockets once during loading; later saves retain their depleted amounts. No offline production is awarded.

## Evidence and limits

The uncut deterministic opening replay reaches its first part at tick 375 (37.5 simulation seconds), with two ore and one part in storage. The ore-funded second-drill replay buys that base at tick 81 (8.1 simulation seconds) and its own routed ore reaches COLLECTION at tick 138 (13.8 seconds). The original fixed-base extension from the processor-first replay reaches new excavation around tick 1191 (119.1 seconds); its kit can be committed earlier while the pocket works. These are simulation trajectories, not human play durations or engagement results. The 30-second communication scenario and review conditions are in the linked 2026-09-23 review.

Tests cover finite terrain and pockets, material conservation, travel and backpressure, courier reservations, the second and third routes, queued kit cancellation, migration, save/resume and undo. Phone-size Playwright screenshots and touch gestures cover the one-gesture feed and full UI path. TypeScript and production build validation are part of the handoff. The existing Phaser/application bundle remains about 1.24 MiB and triggers Webpack's size warning.

The map remains finite. Repeated row layouts and drill-first stockpiling may still be stronger than processor-first construction; no human playtest has resolved that. Output retrieval is through routed belts. The next human sessions should examine whether players notice pocket reserves and blockage, choose to improve processing during the longer production window, and understand queued kit cancellation and the distinct second and third drill sites.
