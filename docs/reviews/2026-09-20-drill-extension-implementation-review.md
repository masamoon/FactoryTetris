# Drill extension implementation: independent adversarial review

20 September 2026. This review challenged the exact bounded implementation, not the general idea of extensible drills. Its PASS authorizes the experiment only.

## Initial verdict: REVISE

The first specification made the extension strictly cheaper while reaching comparable material, so a rational player had little reason to build the fresh drill. That failed the required expansion choice: two buttons are not a meaningful fork when one dominates on cost, routing work and preserved infrastructure.

## Revision reviewed

- The opening replay reaches the decision with exactly one part and two ore.
- Extending consumes one part. It is available only when the first eight-cell shaft is exhausted and every returning load is clear.
- The extension reserves the next complete eight-cell corridor atomically, then a visible tender travels from base to old head before excavation resumes.
- A fresh second drill consumes one part plus two ore and opens a normal placement preview at the authored rich face at x8/y5; the player still commits its route.
- The extension corridor contains 30 ore. The authored fresh corridor contains 32 ore, so both branches produce the same net ore after their different construction costs.
- The fresh site offers shorter initial return distance and an independent base. Extension preserves the existing route and needs fewer construction actions, but keeps the longer return distance, shared output capacity and corridor commitment.
- The experiment permits one extension globally and at most two drill bases. There is no repeatable reach ladder or automatic frontier selection.

## Final verdict: PASS for the bounded prototype

The revision removes the obvious resource-dominance failure and makes the trade legible: reuse and convenience versus a nearer independent extraction point. Exact costs and disabled reasons are shown before commitment. View head and View base controls address the off-screen endpoint cost on a phone. Invalid corridors reject atomically; save/resume and full-state undo preserve the installation and subsequent production honestly.

Deterministic witnesses reach the shared fork at tick 393. The extended drill removes its first new block at tick 443; the fresh branch delivers its first ore at tick 450 after constructing its route. Both branches have equal authored net ore after construction. These measurements prove the implemented causality and cost accounting, not equal fun or globally balanced strategy.

## Remaining risks and evidence needed

- Players may still prefer extension because it requires fewer actions, or prefer a fresh drill because parallel extraction is intrinsically stronger.
- Equal total ore is a narrow authored comparison; terrain topology, downstream capacity and future recipes can reverse it.
- A modal inspector choice may read as an upgrade menu rather than spatial factory planning.
- The extension wait may feel like dead time if the tender and downstream state do not hold attention.
- The global cap postpones rather than solves long-term depletion.
- A silent portrait playtest must verify that viewers identify the selected branch, cost, automatic installation and resulting ore without narration.
- Human tests must compare comprehension, preference reasons, repeated-layout fatigue and recovery from a regretted branch. Automated replays must not be reported as engagement evidence.
