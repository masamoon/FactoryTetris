# Rockhopper Lab projects: adversarial review

Proposal: [../ROCKHOPPER_LAB_PROJECTS.md](../ROCKHOPPER_LAB_PROJECTS.md). Everything below is from code reading and scratch bot runs (simulation, not a playtest). Raw output: [evidence/2026-10-04-lab-projects-round1-belts.txt](evidence/2026-10-04-lab-projects-round1-belts.txt) (`--slow`, fixed costs, crossings on, seeds 1–3; the scratch script was deleted afterwards).

## Round 1

### What the code and the runs show

1. **Tiers 5–6 can't carry more than 4 per bundle with today's caps.** Plates take turns per bundle (`arbitrate` in sim.ts: one bundle holds a gate, whatever its size), so a bigger bundle would pass more items per turn. But `loadBelts` fills a bundle only from what the source holds: a smelter's `ready` (`SMELTER_READY = 4`), a factory's `ready` (`FACTORY_READY = 4`), or a drill's own `buffer` (`DRILL_BUFFER = 4`). Only a drill with input belts or a join can fill more than 4. Every dock-bound belt the bot builds is a smelter or factory output. To test this, every belt at tier 4 or higher was set to tier 6 from 30:00. No bundle went above 4.00 (mean 3.92–3.99 on the saturated belts). Income at 40–60 min went 1568→1668 and 1684→1759 (seeds 1, 2), and 1603→1667 with factories. That +4–6 % comes from runs drifting apart (the bot widened a different belt), not from bundles of 5 or 6. **As written, P2 and P4 do nothing.**
2. **If the caps were raised to the tier, P2 and P4 would become "+% income" nodes and would undo crossings.** The saturated dock belts run full (fill 3.9–4.0) at about 950–1 000 bars/min, which is about half of a tier-4 belt's 1 800/min. They lose loads because bundles back up behind plates. Wait shares are 0.07–0.08 for the bundles that wait at plates (0.03–0.15 across all belts). With 6 per bundle, each turn passes 1.5× as much, so plate-bound belts would gain up to about 50 %. That is a flat income multiplier, priced at 6 400 / 25 600 per belt (4–17 s of late income) and bought on every belt. It also cancels the crossings rule that a tangled factory earns less. The gap between crossings on and off (1.5 K/s against 2.5–3 K/s) would be closed by a purchase, not by layout.
3. **Paired bars/min on the best single belt** (dock-bound belts; 15–25, 30–40 and 40–60 min look alike because income is flat from 15 min):

| Ore     | Best belt, seeds 1–3                                                                    | Bill → minutes on that belt                                 |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Copper  | 188–245 (one belt per seed)                                                             | P2 2 000 → 8–11                                             |
| Ice     | 145–197                                                                                 | P1 1 000 → 5–7                                              |
| Gold    | 90–163                                                                                  | P1 400 → 3–4; P2/P3 800 → 5–9                               |
| Crystal | seed 1: 8 (15–25) / 48 (30–40) / 58 (40–60); seed 2: 43 / 97 / 83; seed 3: 93 / 155 / – | P3 600 → 4–13 (seed 1 at T3 open: over 60); P4 1 200 → 8–21 |

- **P1** (ice + gold): one belt carries both (about 150–197 ice and 84–104 gold/min), so it takes about 6 min. That is plausible.
- **P2** (copper + gold): no belt carries both. The best copper belt carries 0–42 gold, and the best gold belt carries 6–73 copper. Doing one ore and then the other, with a Lab move, takes about 13–20 min, which breaks LP5's 15 min cut.
- **P3 and P4** (crystal): seed 1's crystal belt carries 8/min for 10+ min after T3, so P4 takes about 21 min even at 40–60 min.

4. **The Lab can't clamp onto the busiest belts near the hub.** TT2 needs 43 u from each belt end, so a belt must be longer than 86 u before plate and machine clearances. Lengths in these runs: smelter #4 76 u, factory belts #15 20 u, #16 74 u, #17 74 u. Other dock belts are 131–191 u and keep a window of only 45–105 u, which every nearby plate shrinks further by 29 u on each side. Factory belts #16 and #17 are the busiest (waitShare 0.15).
5. **The bill isn't something to build for.** The belts that carry the most of each ore are the plate-saturated ones (fill 3.9+). A drill or smelter added upstream of one adds about nothing; the plate decides. The belts with room (fill 1.8–2.8) carry less.

### Does it fix dead time?

No. The bot spends 66 % of a 25 min run (seed 1) and 83–85 % of 60 min in gaps over 30 s, saving for slots. A project adds one Lab placement, then 6–20 min of watching a counter, and then 4–17 s of income spent on P2/P4/P3. Only P1's pad docks (241 K–1.6 M) give anything to buy over minutes, and the proposal gates them rather than adding them. The open question "goal or timer?" gets this answer from the data: a timer.

### What the data says the late game is actually about

Income stays flat from 15 min while dock belts sit full at about half their capacity behind plates. Crossings off is worth +60–100 %. **The late-game constraint is the hub knot.** "Untangle the knot" is already the logistics problem the user asked for, but the game neither shows it nor rewards fixing it step by step. Late content should attack that knot through layout, not through a multiplier. Candidates to review separately:

- Ungated pad docks (already S5 PASS).
- An unlockable short bridge/overpass that lifts one belt over one plate (a placed object, priced per use).
- Lab bills whose reward is a new routing piece, not a bigger number.

### Overall: **REVISE** (not adoptable as written)

Required revisions:

1. Drop P2 and P4. If wider belts come back, state the source caps (`SMELTER_READY`, `FACTORY_READY`, `DRILL_BUFFER`). Show with crossings on that they don't close most of the on/off gap, or else reject them as a multiplier.
2. Don't gate pad docks. Either leave them ungated per S5 or change LP6 and the research doc's rejection with reasons. Give P1 a reward that is new and placeable.
3. Rework P3 or cut it. Keep it only with evidence that the crystal reaches the hub, and with a reward that asks for placement.
4. Make every two-ore bill satisfiable on one belt (bot, 3 seeds), or make it one ore. Cut crystal bills until seed 1 makes the 15 min cap.
5. Measure the legal Lab anchor on dock belts. If the busiest belts can't take a clamp, say that placement is "the busiest legal belt".
6. Protect the copper + crystal premium: show that crystal bars reaching factories don't fall, or exclude crystal bills while factories are fed.
7. Change the pass rule from project time alone to project time plus purchases per minute and the share of time in gaps over 30 s from 15 min on, against research alone, with a stated threshold.
8. Re-measure the "Why" baseline with factories on, because research needs Factories on and the quoted numbers are from factories off.

Evidence still needed: the items above, plus a 390 px screenshot of a project row, and the crossings stress tool with projects on (0 locks, 0 stalls).

## Round 2

Revision 2 replaces the projects with Lab-researched bridges (LB1–LB7). Raw output: [evidence/2026-10-04-lab-projects-round2-bridges.txt](evidence/2026-10-04-lab-projects-round2-bridges.txt). Method: the greedy bot (classic field, slow rocks, fixed costs, factories on, 60 min, seeds 1–3). An `onTick` patch removed the N hottest plates by `crossings.ts` heat from the plate list and gates. It ran from 20:00, re-checking every 60 s. Bridges were free and all available at 20:00, so the gains are an upper bound on timing. Scratch scripts deleted. Simulation, not a playtest.

### Measurements

Income per second at 45–60 min (30–45 min in brackets):

| Seed | No bridges    | 1 bridge     | Top 3         | Top 6                 | Crossings off | Share of the on/off gap that 6 bridges recover |
| ---- | ------------- | ------------ | ------------- | --------------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | 1 596 (1 458) | 1 577 (−1 %) | 1 990 (+25 %) | 2 012 (+26 %) (1 949) | 3 628 (3 089) | 20 %                                           |
| 2    | 1 609 (1 613) | 1 596 (−1 %) | 1 671 (+4 %)  | 1 928 (+20 %) (1 895) | 2 720 (2 702) | 29 %                                           |
| 3    | 1 903 (1 632) | 2 016 (+6 %) | 2 005 (+5 %)  | 2 476 (+30 %) (2 414) | 3 338 (3 298) | 40 %                                           |

- **The bot's whole factory has 6–7 plates, 4–5 of them within 150 u of the hub, and the layout is the same for every seed** (classic field: the seed only moves veins). So six bridges cover every plate on seeds 1–2, and six of seven on seed 3.
- **Bridging every plate recovers only 20–40 % of the gap between crossings on and off.** Crossings off is already +44–84 % at 15–20 min, before any bridge would exist. I infer that most of the on/off gap comes from the layout and progression drifting apart (clear lanes, auto-link avoiding plates, an earlier T4), not from plates still waiting. Revision 2's claim that "the knot quietly costs a third to half of late income" should be restated as "bridging every plate is worth +20–30 %".
- **Value is not additive and not ranked by heat.** At 30:00, four hub plates (16:22, 16:36, 17:27, 22:36) all read heat 0.85–1.00. Belt 16 crosses both 22 and 36. Bridging the single hottest plate gains −1 to +6 %, because the next plate on the same belt binds. Three bridges are worth +25 % on seed 1 but +4–5 % on seeds 2–3.
- **Neither copper reaching crystal factories nor the alloys made from it falls with bridges.** Copper delivered in crystal alloys went 0→91, 730→1 685 and 175→1 320 (seeds 1, 2, 3).
- **Plates are hard to tap.** The closest hub plates (16:22 and 16:36) are 23 u apart, which is 13 css px at zoom 0.58. A 44 px target needs zoom about 1.9.
- **Payback.** Six bridges cost 2.52 M and gain about 420–570 credits/s, so the full set pays back in about 75–100 min, longer than the session. Bridges 4–6 alone (2.24 M) pay back in more than 60 min. So bridges are a sink, not an investment. That is acceptable only if the sink is stated as the intent.

### Round-1 revisions

1 met (tiers cut, multipliers banned). 2 met (pad docks not gated). 3 met (drill levels cut). 4 met (one-ore bills, no crystal). 5 partly met (stated, but the anchor measurement is still owed). 6 met, and the measurement shows the premium rising with bridges. 7 met as a rule. My prediction: "time in 30 s+ gaps falls by 10 points" is hard to reach with 6 purchases and 6 short bills over about 30 min, because splitting long gaps doesn't shorten the time spent in gaps over 30 s. Measure it before building UI. 8 met.

### Does it answer the user's question?

It is better than revision 1. The reward is spatial and aims at the measured constraint, and the bot gains +20–30 % with all six. But for the bot's late factory, "where to put six bridges" has one answer (everywhere). The heat cue points to the wrong first bridge about two times in three. And the Lab part is still a 1–4 min timer. The late loop gains about 6 purchases, not "always something worth building".

### Overall: **REVISE**

Required revisions:

1. LB4: set the bridge count from measured plate counts on both fields and with `--messy`, so that a tidy late factory can't bridge every plate, or state openly that it can and why that is the intended reward.
2. LB5: show the chain (the other plates the same belts wait at), give a tap target for plates 13 px apart, and provide the 390 px ghost screenshot.
3. LB1: return a bridge to stock when its plate moves far, and define bridged plates under auto-link and in merged gates.
4. LB2: unlock bridge 1 after the T2 recipes, or measure when it starts per seed.
5. Correct the "Why": bridging every plate is worth +20–30 %, not the whole on/off gap. Re-run the evidence on sectors, where the hub knots more (slow-rocks round 2: 7.2 plates within 150 u).
6. Run the gap-share pass rule with real prices and real bill times before any UI work.
