## Required S5 revision

Begin the clip with the player committing a previewed rig/corridor whose base connects to an existing transport line. Then show actual block removal, chunks returning along the integral rail, and actual delivery into processing. Keep the whole cause-and-effect chain readable in one phone view for this demonstration. Label this a developed-factory clip, not a promised first ten seconds of play.

Treat spatial depth as an untested hypothesis. The rig corridor is occupied infrastructure; possible choices concern source selection, base location and external transport/processing space around it. Do not promise the same mined cells are freely reusable machine floor. A later prototype must author two legal competing arrangements before claiming meaningful spatial tradeoffs.

The parent accepted this revision. Reclaiming drill corridors or dismantling rails is not selected. Historical stable-vein reviews remain records of that earlier scope; this excavation concept does not retroactively validate or rewrite their evidence.

## Concrete failure cases to attack

- **Automation becomes relocation work.** A powerful rig finishes a tiny corridor quickly and needs repeated dismantling/redeployment. Measure useful hands-free production against required redeployment actions over an expansion, rather than celebrating a single unattended drilling animation.
- **Integral haulage bypasses the routing game.** A long-reach rig with no relevant cost can be the best transport across the asteroid. Attack maximum-reach rigs placed to bypass external belts. The integral haulage is coherent, but its eventual reach/cost/capacity must fit the economy.
- **The cheapest strategy stays manual.** If digging by hand removes blocks faster or bypasses construction costs indefinitely, players will keep tapping beside their supposedly automated factory. Compare complete time/material trajectories, not one nominal rate.
- **The mine consumes its own factory footprint.** A rail left in every corridor can clutter the whole outpost. Making that rail vanish instead would imply unexplained haulage. Show occupied space honestly; removal/reuse rules are unresolved.
- **Mining just means point at the largest vein.** Straight shafts with identical yields, transport distances and accessible locations have a universal obvious plan. Multiple colors of ore alone do not establish choices.
- **Transport is secretly global inventory.** Chunks that enter a base must encounter real buffer/full-output limits. Finite terrain, storage and production must conserve materials; an empty block cannot keep yielding ore.
- **Phone control and visibility costs.** Finger occlusion can hide cracks and progress. A wide side-scrolling view can force a choice between seeing the mining face and downstream payoff. The one-screen first-rig demonstration should expose this before adding a larger world.
- **Controls swallow the factory game.** Direct movement can add repeated walking/climbing chores; auto-navigation can create unreachable-target failures or unclear paths. The user's pending control preference changes this evaluation and must not be silently settled by this review.

## Evidence still absent

There is no playable asteroid prototype, tested digging time, selected rig reach, cost curve, material balance, implemented navigation, vertical transport model, or measured player enjoyment. A visually plausible side-view mining loop does not establish that incremental progression stays interesting. The current verdicts do not replace the earlier unresolved progression/economy concerns.
