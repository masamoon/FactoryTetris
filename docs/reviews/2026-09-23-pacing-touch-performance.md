# Asteroid Works pacing, touch and rendering review — 2026-09-23

## Observed problem and evidence

The old starter replay reached its first part at tick 393 (39.3 simulation seconds) after the eight-cell drill had exhausted its shaft. The previous expansion required that part even for a separate new drill. In the old UI, manual feeding meant opening COLLECTION targeting and then tapping a processor, or opening an inspector and pressing its button. The app rebuilt its HUD model every animation frame; the Phaser scene redrew each frame and destroyed/recreated every text object in it. These are code and deterministic-replay observations, not measured human play timings or a phone performance profile.

## Implemented behavior and correctness checks

A real starter replay now earns the first part at tick 375 (37.5 simulated seconds). In a second replay, the first drill returns two ore by tick 81; the player spends that ore on a second base at the prepared x8/y5 face, routes its output, and the second drill's own line ships ore by tick 138. The later x8/y7 base can be funded from real routed ore. A processor-first extension can be queued when the first shaft clears; in the current replay its first newly excavated tile arrives around tick 1191 because the old pocket and loads must drain. That delay is disclosed rather than hidden.

[The drill preview](evidence/2026-09-23-pocket-preview.png) shows a surveyed pocket before purchase. The simulation tests cover pocket conservation, capacity, three routed bases, queued tender travel, cancellation and refund, save migration, semantic validation and complete-state undo. Playwright covers dock drag and one-tap FEED at 360×640 and 390×844, the separate machine inspect tap, the full UI route to parts, queue cancellation, and save continuation. [Held-rock progress](evidence/2026-09-23-hold-progress.png) is drawn from the real work counter on the target. The renderer now caps scene drawing near 30 Hz, reuses Phaser text objects and updates the DOM model on simulation ticks instead of every animation frame. Save serialization runs once per save and skips unchanged paused ticks. Headless Chromium showed about 56 actual Phaser loop frames per second in a 390×844 developed-factory capture. In a separate [three-drill state](evidence/2026-09-23-three-drills.png), it drew 142 frames in five seconds at normal CPU speed and 138 in five seconds under Chrome’s 4× CPU throttle; Phaser’s loop reported about 54 and 35 frames per second respectively. These are headless browser observations, not a physical low-end phone benchmark.

## Remaining questions and next falsifiers

- Does the longer pocket window prompt useful refinery and route decisions, or mainly passive stockpiling? Observe players, not just replay completion.
- Is ore-funded drill-first play stronger than processor-first in actual touch sessions? Compare actions, comprehension and voluntary choices on the same seed.
- Can players see why a queued tender has not launched when the drill output is blocked, and can they find cancellation without losing confidence in undo?
- Are the fixed pockets, repeated straight routes and three-base cap enough for more than this bounded session? The world remains finite; no endless-supply claim is supported.
- Profile a lower-end physical phone with several active machines and a large undo history. The headless 56 FPS observation does not resolve the user's device-specific choppiness.

A small human playtest should start one group at a fresh outpost and another at the disclosed prepared choice. Ask each player to explain where ore comes from, why a drill stopped, what the next build changes, and how to recover a queued extension. Record observed actions and hesitations separately from simulation counters. Treat any proposed timing thresholds as provisional decision rules, not measured industry benchmarks.
