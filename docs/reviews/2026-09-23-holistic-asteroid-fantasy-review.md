# Asteroid Works: mining fantasy and short-form product review — 2026-09-23

## Recommendation

The strongest promise is **choose where to enter an asteroid, make a real cut, and turn the recovered material into a self-running outpost that survives the next geological choice**. The current prototype proves finite excavation and honest transport. Its ordinary path does not yet make _prospecting, changing terrain, and improving the factory_ read as one cause-and-effect loop on a phone.

Rank the next work as: (1) fix the short-phone inspector and preserve the playfield; (2) author a compact, two-seam first bay and try to disprove that its site choice matters; (3) test one genuinely constrained developed factory with two legal responses and sustained downstream output; (4) only then compare linked persistent sectors with separate levels. Asteroid Works already uses simple machine cells. **The two new gameplay tests passed independent adversarial review only as bounded falsification prototypes; no redesign or clip has passed as enjoyable, balanced, or ready to ship.**

## What the current run establishes

The [simulated-run review](2026-09-23-simulated-run-adversarial-review.md) records the full ore → plate → part route. The first automatic excavation occurs at 5.7 simulation seconds, ore reaches collection at 7.8, and the first part arrives at 37.5, excluding human input and paused planning. The starter solution is one straight output row, followed by replacing two belts with processors. A second cheap drill can add ore without advancing the first-part tick. Committing a paid extension at the first-part state takes another 84.4 simulation seconds before the next section excavates.

The [390×844 factory screen](evidence/2026-09-23-run-production-390.png) shows a static-looking rock wall, a thin machine row and a head beyond the right edge. In the [uncut prepared second-drill clip](evidence/2026-09-23-prepared-second-drill-uncut.webm), the second drill genuinely raises ore stock to 68 by the end, while plates and parts remain at zero; the objective stays “Grow or refine.” These observations support a real extraction payoff, but not an easily attributable factory improvement. At [360×640, opening the drill inspector](evidence/2026-09-23-run-inspection-360.png) clips nearly the whole playfield. The present map begins as a long flat face and surveys exact fixed pockets; once a shaft reaches one, much of the following production comes from a stationary head. That is truthful resource extraction, yet visually it can read as an ore faucet more than exploration of a finite body.

The design inference is that the asteroid needs to change the player's _choice_, not only the backdrop. A promising cycle is: **read visible geology → commit a drill and route → watch terrain and material move → use or redirect that material → respond to depletion or congestion**. Discovery should give the player evidence for a decision rather than a blind reward roll. The cavity, rail, remaining seam and processor should explain why one plan worked.

## What looks appealing in short mobile creatives

My reading of the visual grammar is a complete causal sentence: **a legible opportunity or obstruction, one clear intervention, an immediate physical response, then a new consequence**. For mining, a seam glimpsed behind crust promises a reveal; a machine cutting that crust provides motion; cargo reaching a smelter proves that the cut mattered; a narrowing reserve or backed-up route creates anticipation. A large before/after silhouette and an easy-to-follow path communicate more than tiny counters or extra particles.

[TikTok's strategic-game guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/creative-tips-for-strategic-games/pc/en) treats the first 3–6 seconds as a game-related hook followed by clear gameplay. [Unity's playable-ad guidance](https://unity.com/blog/create-retention-playable-ads) likewise stresses that the first interaction must be understandable quickly. These are marketing observations, not proof of player enjoyment or a universal formula. [TikTok's mobile-gaming whitepaper](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Mobile_Gaming_Whitepaper_2024.pdf) explicitly warns that misleading viral ads do not guarantee loyalty. The useful lesson for Gridforge is to make the _actual loop_ produce the appealing sequence, not to speed up or script an unrelated spectacle.

| Creative device            | Truthful counterpart in Asteroid Works                                       | Current gap                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Visible promise            | A finite vein whose route, cost and likely yield can be compared             | Most of the first face is uniform; pocket value is primarily text                        |
| One decisive move          | Commit a drill to one geological prospect or fix a real logistics constraint | Building often means multiple small placements; the extension queues for a long time     |
| Physical payoff            | Rock disappears, a cavity/rail grows, real loads return and enter processing | The distant head and tiny cargo often cannot be seen with the dock                       |
| Before/after system change | Downstream plates/parts or durable capacity improve after the intervention   | The second-drill clip mainly raises stored ore                                           |
| Next question              | Spend, redirect, or prospect before a visible finite seam runs dry           | The objective can acknowledge expansion before its effect or repeat after a second drill |

Only simulation events should drive the visual response. A released backlog must be identified as such; cuts, prepared saves and speed changes need disclosure and an uncut witness.

Presentation should serve that chain: give the selected face, cutter, return load and receiving machine the strongest contrast; leave the excavated cavity visible as a persistent before/after shape; distinguish ore, plate and part by silhouette as well as color; and briefly emphasize the actual route affected by the player's edit. Collapse routine tool chrome while watching production, then show cost and status when a choice is made. An inspector must overlay or resize without hiding the active cut. Decorative dust or glow can emphasize a real break or recipe completion, but must not imply extra material or a faster rate.

## Bounded prototypes that survived adversarial review

### 1. Compact first mining bay: PASS for an authoring test

Two independent reviewers initially returned **REVISE** because “two veins” can still mean one obvious best choice and the current eight-cell shaft cannot fit with the dock in a 360 px view. The revised experiment is a **10-column × 8-row bay at 34 px per cell**, requiring at least a 300 px visible playfield at 360×640 with its inspector closed. This tests a four-cell _starter_ drill section; it does not select that reach as a permanent rule.

The authored topology has COLLECTION at `(0,4)`. A westbound trunk at `(3,4) → (2,4) → (1,4)` delivers ore to it. The initial hand-mined six ore buys one drill at either `(4,3)` or `(4,5)`, with finite rightward corridors through columns 5–8. Its left output turns south from `(3,3)` or north from `(3,5)` into the trunk. After eight earned ore and a drained `(2,4)` belt, that belt can be replaced by a smelter with right input at `(3,4)` and left output through `(1,4)`, as in the present honest bootstrap. The upper seam provides earlier smaller yield; the lower seam provides later richer yield. Exact reserves and hardness are authoring variables to be tested, not balance claims.

The existing two-ore second drill stays in the experiment as an attack: **if “upper first, lower second” is a universal order, the proposed site choice fails**. Same-start replays must include both orders, all costs, return delays, first plate/part times and depletion. A real 360×640 capture must show dock, both faces, drill, cargo and next consequence without shrinking targets under a finger. The candidate clip starts from a **disclosed prepared bootstrap**: six hand-mined ore in storage, the free westbound trunk and both side connections already installed, both seams unmined, and no drill or smelter. In 0–10 seconds the player commits the first drill and its real first load returns; in 10–30 seconds the growing stock presents the actual choice between a cheap second drill and saving for a smelter. It must show the true six-ore cost and may claim a meaningful tradeoff only if the replay and player test establish one. Those timings and silent comprehension remain unverified; this is not a cold-start capture.

### 2. Endogenous throughput constraint: PASS for a simulation/capture test

The developed-factory candidate combines three finite pocket drills into one trunk. At current maximum pocket rates they can release about **0.4 ore per tick**, while a belt transfers at most **one item every three ticks** (about 0.333 per tick). A backlog can therefore arise from the simulation rather than from a scripted “repair this” state. Compare two legal interventions from the same state: a parallel trunk and processing near the source to reduce transport volume. Both must preserve real construction costs, buffers, ore conservation and finite reserves.

This is only worth showing if it improves the _needed downstream product_ over a sustained window after stored cargo drains. Current processor rates are a major counterexample: one smelter uses about 0.1 ore per tick and one assembler produces roughly one part per 31 ticks, so extra transport alone may just create another ore pile. Free belts may dominate paid processing, or carried cargo may make the intended edit illegal. Model enough processor demand, verify both complete legal routes, and abandon or revise the comparison if one intervention always dominates. The candidate 0–10 second hook is a visible, ordinary developed-outpost intervention releasing a real constraint; 10–30 seconds must show durable downstream output and a next constraint, not merely the initial backlog draining. The exact playable starting state and clip are pending that authoring test, so this has no truthful-clip PASS. Any capture must be continuous, real time and labeled as a developed save.

## Structure and deferred alternatives

The asteroid iteration has no tetrominoes or folding. The earlier `?mode=tiles` workshop is a separate historical mode, not a proposal for this loop. The current machine cells are sufficient for the next geology and routing tests.

## Small falsifiable plan

1. Fix the 360×640 inspector/canvas resize failure and verify screenshots after opening each contextual panel. Keep the active cut and its return route visible during normal play.
2. Author only the compact bay as a separate prototype branch or scenario. Record both site-order replays, including the cheap second base. Stop treating seam choice as a feature if one order dominates the relevant opening and developed states.
3. Author the developed bottleneck state only after processor demand is shown to exceed the trunk. Capture each intervention uncut, with parts output over a steady window, visible costs, reserve changes and recovery if a route is full.
4. Run a small muted-phone observation: after 10 and 30 seconds, ask viewers what the player changed, where material went, and what decision follows. Then watch fresh players make the site and route choice without coaching. A provisional rule for continuing is that at least **4 of 6** can correctly explain the action and downstream effect; this is a local decision threshold, not an industry benchmark or a measured result.
5. If the local loop survives, compare three linked sectors with three standalone versions of the same problems. Track repeated route edits, reuse of earlier infrastructure, post-depletion recovery, and whether players voluntarily choose distinct plans. Do not infer retention from this small comparison.

The unresolved questions are whether the cheap second base erases prospecting, whether any real bottleneck admits two useful responses, whether finite geology adds decisions instead of chores, and whether the entire action-to-product chain remains legible under a thumb. A successful silent clip would validate communication of one real event. It would not establish enjoyment, balance, retention or commercial viability.
